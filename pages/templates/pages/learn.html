{% trans "Can I donate?" %}

{% blocktrans %}The blood bank decides. This is a friendly pre-check, not medical clearance. Staff at the blood bank will ask you questions and check you on the day.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "The usual criteria" %}

  • {% blocktrans %}Age 18 to 60. Limits can differ between blood banks.{% endblocktrans %}
  • {% blocktrans %}Weight at least 45 kg.{% endblocktrans %}
  • {% blocktrans %}Feeling well today. No fever, cold or infection, and not recently ill.{% endblocktrans %}
  • {% blocktrans with interval=donation_interval_label %}Gap since your last donation: we use {{ interval }} as a reminder. Blood banks may ask for a different gap.{% endblocktrans %}
  • {% blocktrans %}Tell the staff if you travelled recently, got a tattoo or piercing, take regular medicines, or are pregnant or recently gave birth. They may ask you to wait.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "What happens on the day" %}

  1. {% trans "Register" %}Bring an ID. Staff note your details and ask a few health questions.
  2. {% trans "Quick health check" %}Usually pulse, blood pressure and a small finger-prick test. Answer honestly; it protects you and the patient.
  3. {% trans "Donate" %}You sit or lie down while a trained person collects the blood with a sterile, single-use needle. The donation itself usually takes about ten minutes.
  4. {% trans "Rest and refresh" %}Stay for a short rest with a drink and a snack before you leave. Plan for up to an hour in total.

Steps and timing vary between blood banks. Ask yours if you are unsure.

{% trans "Looking after yourself afterwards" %}

  • Drink extra fluids for the rest of the day.
  • Eat a proper meal, and eat well in the days after.
  • Take it easy. Skip heavy lifting and hard exercise until the next day.
  • If you feel dizzy, sit or lie down and raise your legs. Do not drive until you feel normal.
  • Keep the plaster on for a few hours. If the arm bleeds, press firmly and raise it.
  • Feel unwell later? Contact the blood bank and follow their advice.

{% trans "Myths and facts" %}

Donating makes you weak for weeks.

Most healthy people feel fine after resting and drinking fluids. Your body replaces what you gave over the following days and weeks.

You can catch an infection from donating.

Licensed blood banks use sterile, single-use needles for every donor. Ask to see it opened if you are worried.

It hurts a lot.

Most people feel a quick pinch when the needle goes in, then little else.

Only O blood is needed.

Every group is needed. O- is special because it suits many patients, but patients usually get their own group first.

You should not eat before donating.

Eat a normal meal and drink water first. Donating on an empty stomach can make you feel faint.

Women cannot donate.

Women can donate if they meet the same checks. Some blood banks ask women to wait longer between donations.
